# Goal 5, milestone 3: planned or unexpected?

Generated by `scripts/research_report.py` from queries 27-28 and `reference/outage_labels.csv`.

**Question:** public data labels no outage as planned or forced. Can the way an outage *begins* tell them apart?

## A. Why speed of shutdown doesn't work

| hours_from_90pct_output_to_offline | outages | of_which_announced_refurbishments |
|---|---|---|
| 1. 2 hours or less | 141 | 7 |
| 2. 3-24 hours | 29 | 4 |
| 3. over a day | 30 | 1 |

On hourly data almost every shutdown is "instant", including refurbishments announced years ahead. Speed is not a
usable signal at this resolution.

## B. Three signals that do differ (query 27)

- **Announced step-down:** the capability reported for the last hour before going offline was already below 90% of
  full power. The operator had told the IESO the reactor was coming down.
- **Night start:** the outage began between 20:00 and 04:59.
- **Clean hour:** output in the first offline hour was under 5% of full power, so the reactor was already off when the
  hour began. A trip mid-hour leaves a partial hour of output behind.

An answer key was collected from public sources: 12 outages that CNSC status reports or Bruce Power describe
as planned or forced (`reference/outage_labels.csv`, each row with its source), plus announced refurbishments and
permanent shutdowns, which are planned by definition.

## C. The rule (query 28)

planned points = 2 × announced step-down + night start + clean hour.
- **likely planned:** 3 or more points.
- **likely unplanned:** no announced step-down, at 90%+ output the hour before, not a clean hour, and a daytime start.
- **unclear:** everything else.

Both ends are strict on purpose. Duration and season are deliberately left out of the rule so they can be used to
check it.

| plant | outages_seen_from_the_start | announced_refurb_or_shutdown | likely_planned | unclear | likely_unplanned |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| All reactors | 200 | 12 | 54 | 98 | 36 |
| Bruce | 101 | 3 | 22 | 58 | 18 |
| Darlington | 35 | 4 | 10 | 14 | 7 |
| Pickering | 64 | 5 | 22 | 26 | 11 |

## D. Does the rule work?

Against the sourced outages:

| answer_key | truth | outages | guessed_planned | guessed_unclear | guessed_unplanned |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. used to design the rule | planned | 7 | 5 | 2 | 0 |
| 1. used to design the rule | forced | 5 | 0 | 2 | 3 |
| 2. held back (refurbishments, shutdowns) | planned | 12 | 4 | 7 | 1 |

| check | result | % | 95% low | 95% high |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Design set: sourced PLANNED called 'likely planned' | 5 of 7 | 71 | 36 | 92 |
| Design set: sourced FORCED called 'likely unplanned' | 3 of 5 | 60 | 23 | 88 |
| Held back: refurbishments/shutdowns called 'likely planned' | 4 of 12 | 33 | 14 | 61 |
| Held back: refurbishments/shutdowns called 'likely unplanned' (a wrong call) | 1 of 12 | 8 | 1 | 35 |

(Wilson 95% intervals. They are wide because the counts are tiny.)

Against things the rule never used (outages with no announced refurbishment):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**Interpretation:**
- On the 12 outages the rule was designed around, it never called a planned outage unplanned or the reverse.
  That proves little: the rule was written while looking at them.
- On the refurbishments and shutdowns it had never seen, it called a third "likely planned", most "unclear", and one
  wrong. So **"likely planned" is a cautious label that misses many planned outages**; the announced step-down is a
  specific signal, not a sensitive one.
- **The independent check supports the rule.** Outages guessed "likely planned" last a median of
  50 days; those guessed "likely unplanned", 4.5 days
  (shuffling the labels: p = under 0.001). Long maintenance outages against short fix-and-restart
  outages is what planned against unplanned should look like, and outage length was not in the rule.
- Season points the same way but weakly: 67% of "likely planned" start in January-April or
  September-October against 50% of "likely unplanned" (p = 0.13:
  could be chance).
- Of 200 outages seen from the start, 12 are
  announced, 54 likely planned, 36 likely unplanned and
  98 unclear.

**Limitations:** the answer key is 12 outages, read from search-result excerpts of the CNSC reports (the PDFs
themselves could not be opened from the build environment), so each row should be re-checked against its source
before being relied on. The guesses are *likely*, never *confirmed*. A planned outage that begins abruptly and a
forced one that is brought down gently are both misread by this rule. "Unclear" is half of all outages and should stay
that way until there are more sourced labels.
