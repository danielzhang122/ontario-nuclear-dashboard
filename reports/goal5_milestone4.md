# Goal 5, milestone 4: do reactors go down together?

Generated by `scripts/research_report.py` from query 29.

**Question:** if outages were independent, two reactors would be offline together only by coincidence. Is it more
than coincidence, and is it about sharing a plant?
**Scope:** hours inside announced refurbishments and permanent shutdowns are left out for that reactor.

## A. Hours offline together

**Method:** for reactors A and B, over the hours both are in play: expected together-offline hours =
P(A offline) × P(B offline) × hours. Lift = seen / expected. Pairs at different plants are the control group.
**Shuffle test:** slide each reactor's whole history by a random amount (wrapping around), 1,000 times. Each
reactor keeps its own outages; any alignment between reactors is destroyed. The spread of the shuffled lifts is what
coincidence looks like.
**Result:**

| pair_type | pairs | seen_together_h | expected_by_coincidence_h | lift |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| same plant | 49 | 39299 | 39,638 | 0.99 |
| different plants | 104 | 78755 | 72,982 | 1.08 |

| plant | pairs | seen_together_h | expected_by_coincidence_h | lift | pairs_above_coincidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bruce | 28 | 10709 | 13,729 | 0.78 | 10 |
| Darlington | 6 | 6198 | 3,044 | 2.04 | 3 |
| Pickering | 15 | 22392 | 22,865 | 0.98 | 4 |

| pair type | lift | range from shuffling (95%) | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| same plant | 0.99 | 0.72 to 1.41 | 0.96 |
| different plants | 1.08 | 0.81 to 1.24 | 0.50 |

**Interpretation:** reactors at the same plant are offline together **about as often as coincidence predicts**
(lift 0.99, well inside the shuffle range), and no more than reactors at different plants. Bruce is
below 1: its outages overlap less than chance, as they would if long outages are scheduled one after another.
Darlington is above 1 but has only 6 pairs, so that number rests on very little.

## B. Outage starts close together

**Method:** count pairs of outage starts within 3 days of each other, for same-plant and different-plant pairs. The
same shuffle gives the count expected by coincidence.
**Result:**

| pair_type | pairs | start_pairs_within_3_days | per_pair |
|---|---|---|---|
| same plant | 49 | 21 | 0.43 |
| different plants | 104 | 20 | 0.19 |

Same-plant pairs within 3 days: **21** seen, 13.2 expected by coincidence
(shuffle range 7 to 20), one-sided p = 0.02.

**Interpretation:** outages **begin** together more often than coincidence: 0.43 close pairs per same-plant pair
against 0.19 per different-plant pair. So A and B don't contradict each other. Overlap in hours is ordinary
because long outages are staggered; starts bunch up because a few times a whole plant comes down at once.

**Interpretation:** five events. The April 2024 Bruce B one is confirmed by Bruce Power as a planned **vacuum building
outage**: the reactors at a station share one safety building, and inspecting it requires every connected reactor to
be shut down. The others have the same signature (most of a station down within days, for weeks), so the same
explanation is *likely*, but only the 2024 one is confirmed by a source in this project.
**Limitations:** with 5 events, nothing can be said about how often they recur. The pairwise method can't separate
"shared cause" from "shared schedule". Most outages in the clusters are "unclear" on the planned/unplanned guess,
because a whole-station shutdown doesn't use the announced step-down the rule looks for.
