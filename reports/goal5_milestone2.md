# Goal 5, milestone 2: restarts and maneuvers

Generated by `scripts/research_report.py` from queries 22-26. Data: hourly IESO output and capability, May 2019 to
September 2026. Everything here is **observed electrical behaviour**: what was reported and generated, not what was
happening inside the reactor, and never *why*.

## A. Getting back to full power (queries 22, 23)

**Question:** after an outage of a day or longer, how long until the reactor is really back?
**Definitions:** hour 1 = the first hour after the last offline hour. Three milestones: first hour at 50% of full
power, first hour at 90%, and **steady full power** = the first hour that starts 24 hours in a row at 90% or more.
Each restart ends one of three ways: it *reached* steady full power, it was *interrupted* (the reactor went offline
again first), or it was *censored* (the data or the 90-day follow-up ran out).
**Method:** Kaplan-Meier. At every hour t where a restart reaches steady full power:
share still not back after t = product over all hours up to t of (1 - reached / still being followed).
An interrupted restart counts as "being followed" until the hour it was interrupted and then leaves, so it is
used for as long as we saw it instead of being thrown away. The band is Greenwood's formula. Group differences are
tested with the log-rank test.
**Result:** 198 restarts: 159 reached steady full power, 39 were interrupted.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| All reactors | 198 | 159 | 39 | 19.7 | 9.5 | 31 | 33 | 58 | 126 | 32 | 35.5 | 12.4 | 2,173 |
| Bruce | 103 | 74 | 29 | 28.2 | 7 | 25 | 29 | 53 | 167 | 27.5 | 33 | 9.3 | 1,145 |
| Darlington | 35 | 30 | 5 | 14.3 | 6.5 | 25.5 | 26 | 38 | 76 | 25.5 | 29 | 9.1 | 340 |
| Pickering | 60 | 55 | 5 | 8.3 | 17 | 40 | 41 | 79 | 126 | 40 | 41 | 18.9 | 688 |
| Outage a week to 2 months | 62 | 53 | 9 | 14.5 | 10 | 32 | 34 | 45 | 102 | 33 | 35 | 12.8 | 758 |
| Outage over 2 months | 38 | 26 | 12 | 31.6 | 19.5 | 63 | 71 | 126 | 194 | 67.5 | 90.5 | 26.8 | 557 |
| Outage under a week | 98 | 80 | 18 | 18.4 | 7 | 25.5 | 28 | 39 | 91 | 27.5 | 29.5 | 9.5 | 859 |

`km_*` = hours by which 50% / 75% / 90% of restarts were at steady full power (Kaplan-Meier). `naive_median_h` = the
median of only the restarts that made it. `median_incl_false_starts_h` = counting straight through interruptions.

The curve for all reactors, at a few points:

| 48 | 48 | 4 | 27.9 | 21 | 34.8 |
| 72 | 32 | 1 | 19.7 | 13.5 | 25.8 |
| 91 | 25 | 1 | 15.8 | 10.2 | 21.5 |
| 117 | 17 | 1 | 10.6 | 5.7 | 15.4 |

| 230 | 7 | 1 | 4.9 | 1.4 | 8.4 |
| 282 | 5 | 1 | 3.3 | 0.3 | 6.3 |

**Interpretation:**
- **Half of restarts are at steady full power within 33 hours** of coming back, three quarters within
  58 hours, nine in ten within 126 hours. Half power comes much sooner: a median of
  9.5 hours.
- **The plants differ, and by more than chance** (log-rank chi-square 9.0 on
  2 degrees of freedom, p = 0.01): Pickering's median is
  41 h against 29 h at Bruce and
  26 h at Darlington.
- **Longer outages are followed by slower restarts.** Median 28 h after
  outages under a week, 71 h after outages over 2 months (log-rank
  p = under 0.001). Rank correlation between outage length and time to steady full power:
  0.41 (95% interval 0.20 to 0.56, re-drawing whole reactors).
- **The naive median is close here** (32.0 h against 33 h), because interruptions
  happen early. The gap is in the tail: counting through false starts, the median is 35.5 h.

**Limitations:** Kaplan-Meier assumes an interrupted restart would have continued like the others. That is generous
(restarts that trip are probably the troubled ones), so the true curve lies between the Kaplan-Meier one and the
"including false starts" figure. "Full power" is the reactor's own recent maximum capability (query 15), which can
itself be rising after a refurbishment.

## B. False starts (query 22)

**Question:** how often does a restart not stick?
**Result:**

| group | restarts | interrupted | pct | 95% low | 95% high |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| All reactors | 198 | 39 | 19.7 | 14.8 | 25.8 |
| Bruce | 103 | 29 | 28.2 | 20.4 | 37.5 |
| Darlington | 35 | 5 | 14.3 | 6.3 | 29.4 |
| Pickering | 60 | 5 | 8.3 | 3.6 | 18.1 |

Intervals are Wilson intervals (they behave for small counts).

**Interpretation:** about **1 restart in 5** (20%) goes offline again before
reaching steady full power. Most are brief: the interruption comes a median of 13 hours
after coming back, and in 24 of 39 cases the reactor was then off for less than a day
(median 8 hours). It is far more common at Bruce
(28%) than at the other two plants; shuffling the plant labels gives a difference this
large with p = 0.002.
**Limitations:** the data doesn't say why. A reactor that connects to the grid, disconnects for a test and reconnects
would look the same as one that tripped. Capability dropping to 0 for an hour is all we see.

  the same share of hours. Instead a few bands hold most of them, and each plant has its own. These are "hold
  points". Raising power in steps with pauses for checks is how reactors are normally started, so this is expected,

- **The climb costs energy:** a median of 12.4 full-power hours not made per restart
  (95% interval 10.5 to 16.2), 2,173 GWh over all
  restarts in the data.

and what is the grid doing?

full power, made under 90% of it), using only hours more than 24 hours from any offline hour of that reactor, so
shutdowns and restarts aren't counted.

**Interpretation:**
- **It is a Bruce behaviour:** 341 maneuvers and 2,252 hours at Bruce; the other two
  plants have a handful of 1-3 hour blips.
- **The reduction comes in a fixed step.** 50% of turned-down reactor-hours sit at about 300 MW below the offer.
  A fault would not pick the same number every time; a built-in way of cutting output would. The IESO has described
  Bruce reactors reducing output by about 300 MW each through steam bypass, which fits. That link is *likely*, not
  confirmed by this data.
- **Most maneuvers are one reactor, most of the energy is the big ones.** Maneuvers with two or more reactors carry
  72% of the energy given up.
- **They happen when Ontario has more power than it needs.** Compared with the same kind of hour, demand was
  671 MW lower, the price $3.60 lower, and
  a price of zero or below 16 percentage points more common. Wind
  output was also lower than usual, consistent with wind being curtailed in the same hours. Net exports were not
  clearly different (the interval includes zero).
- **It has become rare:** 921 maneuver hours in 2019 against
  125 in 2025.

**Limitations:** the 90% cut-off means turn-downs of less than 10% of capability aren't counted. Surplus conditions are
inferred from price and demand; the data has no record of a dispatch instruction. The decline since 2020 coincides
with long refurbishments removing reactors from service, which would reduce surplus, but that is not tested here.
